Ion beam

Repulsion

Coulomb treats each trajectory as one ion, feeling every other ion directly — a countable bunch. Beam treats each as a ring of charge and uses Gauss's law on the enclosed current — a continuous beam.

Each drawn particle stands for this many real ions. At 1 the repulsion between a handful of elementary charges is far too small to see — which is the correct answer, and why particle codes weight.

What this model does and does not include

Motion follows m d²r/dt² = q E(r) with E = −∇φ, where φ solves Laplace's equation in the charge-free space between electrodes. Geometry is axisymmetric, so the solve is two-dimensional in (z, r) and rotation supplies the third dimension exactly.

Deliberately absent from this build: magnetic forces, buffer-gas collisions, space charge between ions, image charges, surface roughness and relativistic correction. Each omission is listed with its consequences in docs/PHYSICS.md, along with the known limitations of what is modelled. Results are only trustworthy in the regime where those terms are negligible: dilute, non-relativistic beams in good vacuum.
